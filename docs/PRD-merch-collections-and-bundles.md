# Merch storefront, collections and bundles

## Scope

The first pass implemented the merch storefront redesign and independent collections. The second pass implements bundles ("combos"): fixed-price offers over existing merch products with shared inventory, from the admin editor through checkout, order snapshots, adjustments and exports.

## Storefront direction

Use Glitter's existing identity: ink `#29005c`, purple `#6200cb`, lavender `#eae2ff`, coral `#ff655b`, white `#ffffff`. Gabarito for expressive headings, Figtree for navigation, product names and prices. Left-aligned content, a campaign hero, collection artwork, horizontal collection browsing, then a two/four-column catalog with lightly bordered product cards.

The initial generic shop direction was refined around real collection artwork and names. No invented campaigns, reviews, shipping promises or discount claims. Empty collections are omitted; a store with no products has a deliberate empty state.

Mobbin references inspected:

- [Selfridges seasonal collection sections](https://mobbin.com/screens/b5459f30-58e9-48da-8add-91a1bc80e6b3): image-led entry points with a specific shopping action, followed by products.
- [Urban Outfitters editorial browsing](https://mobbin.com/screens/7a2b59e2-4973-4808-9082-5de52fec353d): distinct visual stories make browsing more than a product grid.
- [lululemon shop-the-look drawer](https://mobbin.com/screens/60199bed-a902-44fd-856e-e5ff03b21d8d): show each component and let customers choose its size. This is a grouped shopping reference, not evidence of bundle discounts.
- [Hers bundle detail](https://mobbin.com/screens/883f4dd8-cdeb-4fae-9c39-17df20367d71): dedicated bundle imagery, contents and a single purchase action. Its subscription pricing is not part of this proposal.

## Collections: implemented behavior

- Collections are independent: name, unique URL slug, description, collection cover, optional campaign banner, publication status, main-banner selection and display order. A festival association is optional.
- Create/edit collections and select products in Admin → Store → Collections. Upload a cover or use an approved image URL. The campaign editor previews the actual banner copy, image, gradient and selected contrast before saving. New collections default to draft. Collection order starts at 1.
- Product Add/Edit also supports collection assignment. Products can belong to multiple collections; unassigned products remain in all merch.
- Public collections contain only visible merch. Empty or unpublished collections do not appear. Festival status does not control publication or artwork.
- Moving a product to supplies removes its assignments. Deleting a festival only clears the optional association; the collection and its products survive. Deleting a product removes its membership rows.
- Only admins can write. Product assignments and collection edits save transactionally; invalid references or duplicate URLs reject the save.
- Each collection has a dedicated `/merch/collections/<slug>` page with its title, description, artwork, products, canonical URL and sharing metadata. Its navigation is a single link back to the store. Search, stock filters and sorting stay scoped to that collection. Clearing filters keeps the collection selected. Product detail links carry the originating store or collection URL and current catalog filters; the product page validates collection membership before offering a return link. Direct product visits fall back to the store. Old `/merch?collection=<slug-or-id>` links redirect to the new page, preserving shopping filters. Unpublished, empty and unknown collections are unavailable. Changing a slug changes the shareable link.
- Lower display-order values appear first, with newest collection ID breaking ties. Admins can select any number of collections for the main banner. Only published nonempty collections are eligible; the selected collections appear in store order and rotate every eight seconds. Shoppers can move backward or forward and pause/resume. Manual navigation pauses rotation; hovering or focusing the banner suspends it, hidden tabs stop it, and reduced-motion users start paused. When none are selected, the first published nonempty collection remains the banner. Search and sorting do not hide it. A campaign image spans the hero on desktop, with a readability overlay behind the text; mobile stacks the image and text. Admins choose dark text and a light overlay for pale images, or light text and a dark overlay for navy/black images. Without a campaign image, the collection cover appears on Glitter’s purple fallback. Card covers stay independent.
- The whole collection banner, not only its button, leads to the collection's products: on the store it opens the collection page at `#catalogo` (`#combos` for a collection holding only bundles), and on the collection page itself it jumps to that section. The button ("Explorar colección" on the store, "Ver productos" on the collection page) stays the only keyboard and screen-reader link; the rest of the banner just widens the click target. The admin preview is not a link.
- Shared collection links show a generated 1200×630 image from the file-based `opengraph-image` route. Next serves it under a hashed name because the route sits in route groups, so the page metadata must not list an image URL of its own; the file convention publishes the right one. A collection with a campaign banner shares that banner laid out like the storefront hero (art on the right, copy over the readability overlay in the admin's chosen text tone). Other collections, and any whose campaign banner cannot be loaded, share the branded purple card with the collection cover, or a "GLITTER" wordmark when the cover cannot be loaded either.
- Existing cart, variant, rental and store-closure rules continue to apply.

Deployment prerequisite: apply migrations through `0289_merch_collection_one_based_order.sql`. Keep migration 0284 intact for existing installations. Migration 0285 copies existing festival collections (name, description and artwork), preserves IDs/product memberships, keeps former draft festival collections unpublished, and advances the ID sequence. Migration 0289 increments existing collection order values while preserving their relative order. New collections have no dependency on festivals. No products are assigned by name inference.

## Bundles: implemented behavior

A bundle is a separately purchasable offer referencing existing products. It has its own identity in the storefront, cart and order, but no duplicate products and no stock of its own.

### Admin workflow

- Admin → Store → Combos lists every bundle with its status (draft, published, published but sold out, published but not shown), price, separate total, saving and distinct product count ("1 producto", "3 productos"). Drafts are checked against the publish rules and show the first thing blocking publication ("Pendiente: …"); published bundles that are not shown give the sale rule they fail ("No se muestra: …").
- The editor sets name, URL slug (`/merch/combos/<slug>`), description, optional cover (upload or approved URL; without one the storefront shows a collage of component images), store order, publication and collections. Collection membership is independent of the components' own collections.
- Components come from a searchable merch product picker. Each has a quantity (1–99) and, for products with variants, a set of eligible variants: one checked variant fixes it, several let the customer choose. A product may appear in more than one component (for example two shirts with separate size choices), but a bundle needs at least two distinct products. No rentals or nested bundles.
- The price is a fixed total in Bs (two decimals). The editor previews the current separate-purchase total, the saving and every blocking problem live, using the same rules the server applies (including which components the same-price rule covers). Unchecking "Publicar" shows the full publish rules again, since republishing a draft needs them.
- Drafts may be incomplete, but every save needs a name, a URL slug, at least one component and a positive price. Publishing a draft (or a new bundle) requires: at least two distinct products; every component visible, purchasable merch; at least one visible eligible variant per variant component; selectable variants of a component sharing one individual selling price; and a positive price strictly below the cheapest separate purchase of the contents. Separate totals use current selling prices, including product discounts, never list prices.
- While a bundle stays published, the same-price rule covers only components the save adds or whose product, quantity or eligible variants change; unchanged components are held to the sale rules. A published bundle whose variant prices diverged can still be renamed, repriced, reordered, given a new cover or moved between collections without unpublishing it.
- Saves are transactional and lock the bundle row. The editor submits the save token it loaded (the row's last update time); a save from an editor that loaded an older copy, or an update without a token, is refused with "Otra persona guardó este combo mientras lo editabas. Recargá la página para ver sus cambios." Component rows keep their ids across edits; switching a component to another product replaces it.
- `version` increases whenever the price or contents change (not for name, description, cover, order, publication or collections). Carts holding an older version must be confirmed before checkout.
- Deleting a bundle locks its authenticated cart lines, then the bundle (checkout's lock order, so it never deadlocks a checkout), and removes it from those carts; guest carts drop it at their next resolution (see Carts). In the brief window where a checkout holds a line added meanwhile, the delete fails with "Alguien está comprando este combo en este momento. Intentá eliminarlo de nuevo." Orders keep their snapshots (`order_bundles.bundle_id` becomes null).
- Products used by a bundle cannot be deleted, and their eligible variants cannot be removed, until they leave the bundle. A product ever sold inside a bundle can never be deleted, even after it leaves the bundle, individually or in bulk; hide it instead ("Este producto se vendió dentro de un combo y esos pedidos lo necesitan. Ocultalo de la tienda en lugar de eliminarlo."). A variant with any order lines cannot be removed, so past bundle orders never lose component lines.

### Runtime availability

A published bundle is shown and sold only while it still passes the sale rules against the live catalog: every component visible, purchasable merch with an available eligible variant, and the price below the cheapest separate purchase. If a component is hidden, moved to supplies, stops being purchasable or its price drops so the bundle is no longer a discount, the bundle disappears from the storefront and cart lines report it as unavailable. The admin list shows the reason. Divergent variant prices introduced after publishing are tolerated as long as every combination remains discounted.

A bundle is "Agotado" ("Publicado · Agotado" in the admin list) only when no combination fits stock. A combination takes one sellable eligible option per component, and its demand is summed per stock pool across all components, fixed and chosen, so two components drawing on one product's stock are counted together. The check is an exact search; a pathological bundle that exceeds 100,000 search steps is reported as available, and cart and checkout still check the combination actually chosen.

### Customer experience

- `/merch` shows a "Combos" section when sellable bundles exist. Collection pages show their assigned bundles in "Combos de la colección". A collection holding only bundles counts as nonempty; its page shows the bundles without an empty product catalog.
- Cards show the cover or collage, the item count and the distinct product count ("1 producto"), the fixed price, the cheapest separate total struck through and "Ahorrás Bs X" ("Ahorrás desde" when selectable variants differ in price), and "Agotado" when no combination has stock.
- The bundle page (`/merch/combos/<slug>`, with canonical and sharing metadata; the description reads "Combo de N productos por BsX") shows the cover, price, separate total and saving for the chosen combination, each included product with quantity and a link, fixed variant labels, pill selectors for choices (sold-out options disabled), pre-sale notes, availability ("Última unidad disponible." when one bundle is left), quantity (up to 5 per cart line) and one add-to-cart button. It starts on the first in-stock option of each choice; if that combination cannot be bought together (components of one product competing for its stock), it preselects one the stock can serve. Its back link returns to the store or to a collection that lists the bundle, validated like product return links.
- The cart shows one grouped line per bundle configuration with expandable contents, the fixed price and separate total. Quantity changes or removal apply to the whole bundle; buying fewer components means buying them individually at their own prices. Bundles never form automatically from individual lines, and the store has no coupon codes to stack.

### Carts

- Authenticated carts store `cart_bundles` (bundle id, version seen, quantity and a canonical selection key) plus `cart_bundle_selections` (component → variant). The key and the stored selections follow the bundle's current definition: only components that are currently a choice count, so a pick for a fixed component is dropped. Adds merge by that key, re-keying a matching line stored under an older one. Guest carts keep the same identity in local storage (`glitter_guest_cart_bundles`) plus a non-authoritative snapshot: display data and each component's product and variant, which the browser uses to cap the guest's individual adds (see below). The snapshot never reaches the server, which resolves guest lines from line key, bundle id, version, quantity and choices alone.
- Every read re-resolves bundle lines on the server: current price and contents, the customer's choices and stock. Stock is shared: each line's limit subtracts the individual lines and all other bundle lines in the cart, and individual lines' limits subtract bundle demand, so two lines never count on the same last unit. Lines are flagged `unavailable`, `selection_invalid`, `stale` (version changed), `out_of_stock` or `stock_insufficient`, and checkout stays blocked until they are fixed. `unavailable` and `selection_invalid` lines reserve no stock. An `unavailable` line shows no price in the cart or checkout summary and is left out of their totals.
- An unpublished bundle never shows its name, price or contents in any cart, signed-in or guest: it appears as "Combo no disponible" with no price and can be removed.
- "Aceptar precio actual" confirms only the version the row showed; if the bundle changed again, nothing is confirmed ("El combo volvió a cambiar. Revisá el nuevo precio."). Confirming re-keys the line and merges it with a current line of the same configuration, up to 5 units ("Juntamos las líneas iguales de este combo. Podés llevar hasta 5 unidades." when units are dropped). While a line awaits confirmation, adding more of it is refused ("Este combo cambió desde que lo agregaste. Confirmá su precio actual en el carrito antes de agregar más.").
- Guest bundle adds go through the server with the signed-in rules and messages: shared stock, 5 per line, refusal of changed versions and unconfirmed lines, and store closure. A partial add says why: "Agregamos N: podés llevar hasta 5 unidades de este combo." when the line cap held it back, "Agregamos N por el stock disponible." when stock did. A guest cart holds at most 20 distinct bundle configurations ("Podés tener hasta 20 combos distintos en el carrito.").
- Guest resolution checks lines one by one: a malformed line, or one past the 20th, is flagged and blocks checkout while the others still resolve. A bundle the admin deleted leaves the guest cart at the next resolution ("Quitamos de tu carrito un combo que ya no existe."). Resolution also returns each individual line's limit after bundle demand; the guest quantity selector, stock warnings and checkout button use it, and guest checkout validates individual lines even without bundles.
- Guest individual adds (product page and store cards) cap the line at its stock minus what the guest's bundles take from the same pool, and at 5; an add never lowers an existing line, and one that finds no room says "No hay stock disponible."
- After a guest order, the payment page clears both guest cart keys, and open store tabs re-read them, so a reload never brings back ordered bundles.

### Checkout and orders

- Browsers send only bundle id, the version they saw, quantity and choices. Checkout share-locks the bundles, then locks every product and variant row in id order (individual lines included), re-validates publication, merch category, purchasability, variants, current prices, discount and store closure, and rejects stale versions (`bundle_changed`) or unavailable bundles (`bundle_unavailable`).
- Demand from individual lines and bundle components is aggregated per stock pool before deducting; deduction reuses the conditional stock updates, so any failing component rejects the whole order. Concurrent checkouts of the last unit sell it once. The pre-check keys component demand by pool, product and variant, so a shortage in the sale pool reads "X del combo Y - stock insuficiente" even when the cart also holds a separate-stock rental line of the same product.
- The bundle price is split across component units in integer cents, proportionally to their individual selling prices. Largest-remainder rounding gives leftover cents to the units with the largest remainders (earlier components win ties), so a component's units may receive two amounts one cent apart; each amount becomes its own order line. Allocations always add up to the exact bundle price and never exceed a unit's individual price. Example: shirt Bs100 + tote Bs60 + 2 × stickers Bs10 = Bs180, sold for Bs150 → shirt Bs83.34, tote Bs50.00, stickers Bs8.33 each.
- Component lines are regular `order_items` priced at their allocation, so order totals, cancellation stock restoration, adjustments, returns, profitability and product totals use what was actually paid. `order_bundles` snapshot the bundle (id, version, name, slug, image, quantity, unit price, separate price and exact total in cents); `order_bundle_items` link each component line with its units per bundle, individual price and paid allocation in cents. Editing or deleting a bundle never changes an existing order.
- Customer order edits change bundles only as whole units: fewer or none, never more, and never single components. Each bundle may appear once, with an integer quantity between 0 and its whole-bundle quantity; duplicate or malformed entries are rejected ("El ajuste contiene combos inválidos."). An edit that removes every remaining line cancels the order in the same transaction (new revision, a `cancelled` event with reason `emptied_by_adjustment`, no second stock restoration, no email) and the form returns to `/my_orders`. Admin adjustments may reduce component lines (refunding their allocation) but not increase them; extra units are added as individual products at their own price. An admin reduction to Bs0 does not cancel the order. Merchandise returns refund each component's paid allocation.
- Adjustments and returns take back what the order actually charged, to the cent. Each change is rounded once as a whole, the same way checkout rounded the order total, never unit by unit: removing 2 × Bs10.625 (Bs12.50 at 15% off) takes back the Bs21.25 checkout charged, not Bs21.26. A product added to an order is priced in whole cents, so removing it later takes back exactly what it added. An edit or return that leaves nothing to pay (no lines left, or only free ones) brings the order to exactly Bs0, never a cent above or below; refunds equal what was actually paid. Bundle allocations are whole cents, so removing, returning or reducing bundle components takes back exactly their allocations. A return always refunds exactly the amount the order total went down.
- Order pages (customer, guest, payment and admin) group bundle lines under the bundle name with its price; the admin view also shows each component's individual price. The customer edit form shows one bundle's contents per row ("Cada combo incluye: 1 × Polera (Talla: M), 1 × Tote, 2 × Stickers"), merging a component split across two allocation amounts; a bundle the store already adjusted shows "Contenido actual" with the remaining units. Compact lists and dialogs label bundle lines with their bundle. Confirmation emails list each bundle with its contents. The line CSV export adds `bundle_line_id`, `bundle_name` and `individual_unit_price_bs`; the summary export marks bundle lines.

### Schema

Migration `drizzle/0290_merch_bundles.sql` adds `merch_bundles`, `merch_bundle_components`, `merch_bundle_component_variants`, `merch_bundle_collections`, `cart_bundles`, `cart_bundle_selections`, `order_bundles` and `order_bundle_items`. Composite foreign keys keep eligible variants tied to their component's product and allocation rows tied to lines of the same order; checks enforce positive quantities, discounted order prices, `total = unit × quantity` and allocations within individual prices. Migration `drizzle/0291_merch_bundle_selection_component_idx.sql` adds a non-unique index on `cart_bundle_selections(component_id)`, so every foreign key on the bundle tables has an index leading with its column (a database test enforces this). It speeds up cascades and restricts from `merch_bundle_components` and changes no behavior.

Deployment prerequisite: apply migrations through `0291_merch_bundle_selection_component_idx.sql`. Both migrations only create tables and an index; no existing data changes.
